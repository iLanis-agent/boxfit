How the dim-weight tax works

Carriers price space, not just mass. Multiply your box sides for cubic inches, divide by the carrier's divisor (UPS and FedEx: 139, every box; USPS: 166, only above 1,728 cu in), round up - that is your dimensional weight. You pay for whichever is higher: scale or dimensional. A 24x18x18 pillow box is 56 "pounds" to UPS no matter what the scale says. The fixes: shrink the box, crush the void fill, split into two smaller boxes, or ride a flat-rate box where weight stops mattering entirely.
